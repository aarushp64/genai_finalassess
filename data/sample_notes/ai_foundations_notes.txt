## 1. Generative vs. Discriminative Modeling
Discriminative models estimate the conditional probability distribution P(Y|X), mapping observable features X directly to target class labels Y. Examples include Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and standard deep convolutional classification backbones.
In contrast, Generative models estimate the joint probability distribution P(X, Y) or the marginal data distribution P(X). By modeling the underlying data generation process, generative models can generate new synthetic data points x_new ~ P(X). Examples include Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Diffusion Probabilistic Models, and Autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs).

## 2. Prompt Engineering Strategies
- Zero-Shot Prompting: Providing a direct instruction without input-output exemplars. Relies entirely on pre-trained semantic representations.
- Few-Shot Prompting (In-Context Learning): Supplying k exemplary input-output demonstrations before the final query. Conditions the autoregressive attention mechanism to follow demonstrated formatting and reasoning styles without updating model weights.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting: Prompts the model to break complex queries into intermediate symbolic steps before emitting the terminal answer. Substantially improves logical accuracy in multi-step mathematical and algorithmic derivations.

## 3. Temperature Scaling in Softmax Decoding
During autoregressive generation, next-token logits z_i are converted to probabilities p_i via the temperature-scaled softmax function:
p_i = exp(z_i / T) / sum_j exp(z_j / T)
- T = 0.0 corresponds to greedy decoding (argmax), always picking the highest-logit token deterministically.
- T = 0.7 balances coherence and vocabulary diversity for educational dialogue.
- T > 1.0 flattens the probability distribution, increasing output entropy and randomness.
