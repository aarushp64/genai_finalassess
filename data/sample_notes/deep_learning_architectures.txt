## 1. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)
Formulated by Goodfellow et al. (2014), a GAN consists of two competing networks engaging in a zero-sum minimax game:
- Generator G(z; theta_g): Maps prior noise z ~ p_z to synthetic data G(z).
- Discriminator D(x; theta_d): Evaluates probability that sample x is from real distribution p_data rather than synthetic p_g.
The minimax objective function is:
min_G max_D V(D, G) = E_{x ~ p_data}[log D(x)] + E_{z ~ p_z}[log(1 - D(G(z)))]
When the discriminator is optimal, this minimizes the Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence between the real and generated data distributions.

## 2. Common GAN Failure Modes
- Mode Collapse: The generator collapses to produce only a tiny subset of distinct realistic samples, failing to cover the full variance of the data distribution.
- Vanishing Gradients: When the discriminator learns too quickly, log(1 - D(G(z))) saturates and gradients vanish, stalling generator updates. Mitigated via non-saturating loss -log D(G(z)) or Wasserstein GAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP).

## 3. PyTorch Computational Graphs & Autograd
PyTorch utilizes reverse-mode automatic differentiation over dynamically constructed Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs).
During the forward pass, PyTorch builds execution graphs tracking tensor operations.
During loss.backward(), the multivariate chain rule propagates partial derivatives dL/dW through each parameter tensor, allowing gradient descent updates:
W = W - eta * (dL/dW)
